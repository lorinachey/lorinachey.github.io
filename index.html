Thesis research

Competence and risk awareness

Robots and AI agents should know what they are competent to do and take risk tolerance as an explicit input, and both should still hold when the system is compressed, physically embodied, degraded, or handed to a new user. I study where those guarantees break between calibration and deployment, and build agents whose risk behaviour stays calibrated. One part of this uses distributional reinforcement learning to learn quadruped locomotion and navigation policies that account for risk directly.

Secondary research interests

Generative occupancy for exploration

Diffusion models that complete a 3D scene from partial observation, so a robot can plan against what is probably there rather than only what it has already seen.

Read more →

Embodied scene understanding

Predicting where a carried object belongs in a home from the room layout, the person carrying it, and what little is known of their routine, so a robot can tidy or assist without being told where things go.

Multi-agent coordination

Planning where and when a team of robots should transmit when the radio does not reach, so agents share what they have found without backtracking to meet.
